How single-trace attributes actually work
Modules About AASPI

AASPI  ·  University of Oklahoma

These attributes never look sideways. One trace is all they get.

Dip, coherence and curvature all compare a trace with its neighbors. The attributes in this set do not. Each one takes a single trace, on its own, and asks a question about its shape: how strong is it here, where in the wavelet am I, how fast is it wiggling, how much energy is in it. That is a severe restriction, and it is also why they are fast, why they are on every interpretation system, and why they know nothing whatever about structure.

Two pieces of machinery answer nearly all of it. Turning the trace into a rotating arrow gives you envelope, phase and frequency. Sliding a running window down the trace gives you RMS amplitude, AGC and everything built on them. Six modules, each one thing at a time, computed live in your browser.

One trace, no neighbors

Nothing in this set can tell a fault from a flat spot, because nothing in it ever looks at the trace next door. What you see on a map of one of these attributes is bedding, tuning and gain.

Two engines, six attributes

The complex trace gives envelope, phase and frequency. A running window gives RMS amplitude and AGC. The remaining attributes are combinations of those two ideas, not new ones.

Amplitude is a decision

Most of these attributes scale directly with the numbers going in. Change the gain upstream and the map changes everywhere, without anything in the ground having moved.

The modules

In the order they build on each other. The first one is the foundation: once the complex trace makes sense, four of the remaining five are variations on it.

What you need first. You should know that a seismic trace is a record of reflections, that a reflection happens where acoustic impedance changes, and that impedance is velocity multiplied by density. Nothing else is assumed. Every other term is explained where it first appears, and there is no mathematics you have to do yourself.

What you will be able to do afterwards. Say what each of these six attributes measures and what it cannot see; recognize when an anomaly is geology and when it is a window length, a wavelet or a gain decision; and give more than one explanation for an amplitude anomaly, which is the habit the exercises are built around.

AGC

Divide the trace by its own RMS amplitude and every event becomes equally bright. Useful for picking, and the fastest way there is to destroy an amplitude interpretation.

What this is, and what it is not

These modules are teaching models, built to make ideas visible. The attribute algorithms are implemented as the AASPI documentation and the original papers describe them, but on small synthetic traces and in their plain form, so the method can be read and watched rather than assumed. Each module lists what it has left out and where it departs from production software.

This is not attribute software. For production work on real volumes, use AASPI or your interpretation package. The numbers here describe the model on the screen, not your survey.

About

Companion to How Geometric Attributes Actually Work, which covers the attributes that do look sideways — dip, coherence and curvature — and to What Can You REALLY See in Seismic? on resolution. This set is the one to read first: it is the shortest path from a wiggle to an attribute.

Everything runs in the browser, with no installation and no account. The one thing recorded is that a page was opened. I keep that count so the modules people actually use are the ones that get improved, and so I can show the university these are being used — which is how they keep getting built.
